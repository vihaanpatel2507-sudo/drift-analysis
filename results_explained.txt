 PROJECT RESULTS — PLAIN-ENGLISH EXPLANATION (PHASE BY PHASE)
 For: SAML-D Anti-Money-Laundering Drift Detection
 (Use this file to explain your project to anyone in 5 minutes.)
=====================================================================

WHAT THE PROJECT DOES IN ONE SENTENCE
-------------------------------------
We train a fraud-detection model on the PAST, then watch it run into the
FUTURE to see (1) if it gets worse, (2) how fast we notice, and (3) how
well retraining fixes it.

THE SETUP (understand this first)
---------------------------------
- We split 9.5 MILLION transactions into 10 time-ordered chunks of about
  950,000 each. Chunk 1 = the past (training). Chunks 2-10 = the future.
- Fraud is rare in every chunk: only ~900-1,100 fraudulent rows per chunk
  (about 0.1% = 1 in 1,000). That is the reality of banking data.
- "F1 score" = the main score. It balances catching fraudsters (recall)
  against not crying wolf on honest customers (precision). Higher = better.
  Because fraud is so rare, F1 numbers LOOK small (0.05-0.20) — that is
  NORMAL and expected in AML; don't be embarrassed by it.

=====================================================================
PHASE C — DOES THE MODEL GET WORSE OVER TIME?  (Answer: Yes, badly)
=====================================================================
We trained an XGBoost model on chunk 1 ONLY (past), froze it, then ran it
on chunks 2-10 (future). We also did each run 3 times with different
random seeds to make sure the result isn't luck.

RESULT (F1, average across 3 seeds):
  Chunk 1 (training era, held-out):  0.178   <- model is good here
  Chunk 2  (near future):            0.083
  Chunk 3:                           0.077
  Chunk 4:                           0.104
  Chunk 5:                           0.101
  Chunk 6:                           0.074
  Chunk 7:                           0.064
  Chunk 8:                           0.053
  Chunk 9:                           0.076
  Chunk 10 (far future):             0.107

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PLAIN WORDS:
- The model is DECENT on its own time period (F1 ~0.18).
- The instant the future arrives, performance CRASHES to about 0.05-0.11
  and stays low. That is a 40-70% relative drop (~54% measured against
  the average of the future chunks).
- ROC-AUC (a separate, more technical score that ignores the threshold)
  also drops from ~0.89 to 0.69-0.73, proving the model really got worse
  at RANKING — it's not just a bad cutoff, it's genuine decay.
- WHY this happens: SAML-D introduces NEW fraud patterns over time that
  the training era never saw. So "what laundering looks like" changes.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY:
  A model trained on the past loses 40-70% of its quality as the future
  arrives. Concept drift is real and severe here.

=====================================================================
PHASE D — CAN WE NOTICE THE DAMAGE?    (Answer: partially, without labels)
=====================================================================
We ran 5 label-free "smoke detectors" to see if the model's decline could be
spotted, all of them without a single fraud label:

  Detector 1: PSI on 6 derived numeric input features
             (amount, log-amount, hour of day, day of week,
              transactions-per-sender, average-amount-per-sender)
     Result: saw NOTHING (0 of 9 chunks; worst PSI anywhere = 0.036,
     far below the 0.20 alarm).
     Meaning: the transaction data's own distributions look almost
     identical over time — the "inputs" barely move.

  Detector 2: PSI on categorical inputs (currencies, bank locations,
             payment type)
     Result: saw NOTHING (0 of 9; worst PSI = 0.0038).
     Meaning: category mix is unchanged. This is why detector 1 is not
     just an oversight — adding behavioural features genuinely didn't help.

  Detector 3: KS test on the model's predicted probabilities
     Result: fired on 7 of 9 future chunks.
     Meaning: the model's own confidence scores shift slightly — a label-
     free warning, but see the honesty note below.

  Detector 4: PSI on the same predicted probabilities
     Result: saw NOTHING (0 of 9; worst PSI = 0.00019).
     Meaning: how MUCH the scores moved is practically nil.

  Detector 5: Domain classifier (XGBoost that tries to tell "old" rows
             from "new" rows, 3 seeds)
     Result: fired on ALL 9 future chunks, AUC 0.58-0.61 (mean ± 95% CI).
     Meaning: given derived behaviour features, a classifier CAN tell the
     future data apart — the strongest and most stable label-free signal.

HONESTY NOTE (important, and worth saying in a paper):
  Detectors 3 and 4 disagree, and the reason is sample size. With ~1,000,000
  rows per chunk, the KS critical value falls to about 0.002, so KS rejects on
  differences that are statistically detectable but practically meaningless.
  KS is a SENSITIVITY test; PSI is an EFFECT-SIZE test. Reporting only KS
  would overstate the drift; reporting only PSI would understate it. We
  report both, and the truth is "the output distribution moves, but not
  by much."

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PLAIN WORDS:
- The drift is HARD to see from the raw data alone (inputs barely move),
  but it IS recoverable without labels if you look in the right place:
  a behaviour-feature domain classifier flags every future chunk.
- The most direct, strongest signal is still the labeled F1 drop.

=====================================================================
PHASE E — IS THE DECAY A LABEL ILLUSION?   (Answer: No, it's real drift)
=====================================================================
Someone might argue: "The model only looks bad because you measured it
with few labels." So we tested that fairly.

We measured the frozen model's F1 on future chunks while only a fraction
of labels were available (100% down to 0.1%), and compared against a
MATCHED "no-drift control" — the same label budget, but evaluated on
chunk 1 where there is NO drift.

RESULT (future chunks, F1):
  Labels available | F1 on drifted future  | No-drift control F1 | Future/Control
  100%             | 0.092                 | 0.170                |  0.54
  50%              | 0.094                 | 0.184                |  0.51
  10%              | 0.088                 | 0.166                |  0.53
  5%               | 0.075                 | 0.131                |  0.57
  1%               | 0.114 (3/3 seeds)     | only 1/3 seeds scored|  not comparable
  0.1%             | too few positives     | too few positives    |  not measurable

A ratio is only printed when ALL THREE control seeds produced a real score.
Below that threshold the comparison is meaningless, so we leave it blank
instead of dividing by a number that was never measured.

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PLAIN WORDS:
- At every budget we can actually measure (100% down to 5%), the drifted
  future data scores only 51-57% as well as the no-drift control. So the
  decay is GENUINE DRIFT, not a measurement artifact.
- Label scarcity itself costs very little: the drifted arm's F1 is
  essentially flat from 100% down to 5% labels (0.092 -> 0.075, within
  overlapping 95% confidence intervals). Running out of labels mostly
  stops you from REPAIRING, not from DETECTING.
- Below 5% we stop answering the question rather than guessing. At 1%,
  only ~10 fraud examples are visible per chunk and 2 of the 3 control
  runs found too few positives to score at all; at 0.1% (about 1 positive
  per chunk) neither arm can be scored. The honest statement is "the
  measurement breaks down here", not "drift disappeared".
- Below 0.1% labels there are too few positives to score at all, so we
  honestly report "not enough data" (NaN) instead of a fake 0.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY:
  The drop is real drift — at every reliable label budget the future scores
  barely half of a no-drift control, and it is not caused by having few labels.

=====================================================================
PHASE F — DOES RETRAINING FIX IT?   (Answer: Partially, if done right)
=====================================================================
Every strategy was tested OUT-OF-SAMPLE: a model trained on chunks
1..c-1 is scored on the NEXT unseen chunk c (never its own data).

  Strategy                       | Mean F1 (chunks 2-10) | Final chunk
  no_retrain (frozen baseline)   |   0.092               |   0.118
  periodic_full (retrain on ALL  |   0.114  (+28%)       |   0.155
      history, natural weights)  |
  periodic_sliding (last 2       |   0.106               |   0.135
      chunks only)               |
  periodic_weighted (naive       |   0.030   (WORSE!)    |   0.023
      10x up-weight)             |

We also swept the positive-class weight w in {1,10,50,100} (on top of the
dataset's natural imbalance ratio). Averages over chunks 2-10:

  w    | Mean F1  | Precision | Recall  | Beats other w on
  1    | 0.1004   | 0.5470    | 0.0579  | 9 / 9 chunks
  10   | 0.0212   | 0.0112    | 0.2116  | 0 / 9
  50   | 0.0076   | 0.0038    | 0.4134  | 0 / 9
  100  | 0.0032   | 0.0016    | 0.5588  | 0 / 9

  ("Beats other w on" compares the four multipliers against each other.
   Even w=1 trails the frozen baseline on chunks 2, 3 and 7 — beating a
   re-weighting scheme is not the same as beating no retraining at all.)

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PLAIN WORDS:
- Periodic full-history retraining is the ONLY reliable repair: about
  +28% relative F1 (0.092 -> 0.114; final chunk 0.118 -> 0.155).
- Forgetting old data (sliding window) helps a bit but not as much as
  keeping all history when drift is mild.
- Correct class weighting matters: over-weighting is a precision-killer.
  Pushing w up keeps buying recall (0.058 -> 0.559) and pays for it in
  precision (0.547 -> 0.0016), so F1 falls monotonically. Boosting a class
  that is 1-in-1,000 doesn't make the model better at RANKING fraud, it
  just drags the decision threshold until almost every alert is wrong.
  "Just boost the fraud class" is actively harmful here.
- Fair to the weighted runs: their PR-AUC is still slightly BETTER than the
  frozen baseline. The damage is confined to the thresholded decision, not
  to the quality of the underlying scores.
- Even the best strategy does NOT fully restore training-era F1 (0.178).
  Retraining recovers a meaningful chunk of the loss cheaply, but labels
  alone can't fully undo a change in WHAT laundering looks like.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY:
  Periodic, full-history, correctly-weighted retraining recovers ~28% of
  the lost quality cheaply — but can't fully reverse the drift.

=====================================================================
THE WHOLE STORY IN 4 BULLETS (memorise these)
=====================================================================
1) C — The model genuinely decays: F1 fell 40-70% (0.18 -> 0.05-0.11) as
      the future arrived. Real drift, and real loss of ranking power.
2) D — The drift is hard to see in raw data (all input PSI: 0/9), but a
      behaviour-feature domain classifier flags ALL 9 future chunks without
      a single label. Best signal is still the labeled F1 drop.
3) E — The decay is real drift, NOT a label illusion: a matched no-drift
      control scores ~1.8-2.0x higher than the drifted future at every budget
      where the measurement is trustworthy (100% down to 5%). Label scarcity
      itself costs almost nothing; below 5% the measurement stops being
      possible, and we report that instead of a number.
4) F — Repair works only if done right: periodic full-history retraining
      recovers +28% relative F1 out-of-sample; over-weighting the fraud
      class actively makes it worse.

BOTTOM LINE: AML concept drift is severe, only partly visible without
labels, and bounded by label availability — but a correctly-weighted,
periodic full retrain is a cheap and worthwhile repair.
